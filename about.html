About Steganography

Definition: Steganography hides the existence of a message by embedding it in a “cover” medium (image, audio, video, text).

History: From ancient hidden ink and shaved-head messages to modern digital LSB methods and watermarking.

Why it matters: It enables covert channels (good: privacy, watermarking; bad: malware exfiltration), making it a key cybersecurity topic.

Core Concepts

  • ⌁Cover: the innocent-looking file.
  • ⌁Payload: the hidden message/data.
  • ⌁Stego object: cover + payload combined.
  • ⌁Key (optional): controls embedding/extraction.

Steganography in Cybersecurity

In modern environments, steganography is used both for defense (watermarking, tamper detection) and attack (covert channels, data exfiltration). Understanding it helps defenders detect hidden signals inside “normal” traffic.
